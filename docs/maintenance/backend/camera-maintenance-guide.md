# Camera maintenance

## Ownership and recovery

`CameraService` is the public coordinator. `camera_runtime.py` serializes device selection, connection, recording and stop requests. `camera_worker.py` is the only code that opens the selected camera or its recording writer. The helper is a Windows spawned process, so a blocked native driver call can be recovered without restarting RobotControl.

Recovery is manual. Stop requests allow 15 seconds for clip finalization, then may terminate only the helper. Its exit must be verified before replacement. Every helper owns fresh IPC resources and a generation ID. Old frames/events cannot enter a replacement connection. The main application retains scheduling, SQL monitoring, archive coordination and viewer sessions.

The preview transport has one fixed 640x480 colour frame slot. Recording remains in the helper and does not depend on a browser. Clip events use acknowledgements; completed clips also have durable JSON sidecars so they can be recovered after a parent interruption. Do not introduce frame queues or open devices from API handlers.

Live view is **H.264 only** (owner decision 2026-10-01; brief in `docs/plans/h264-live-view-spike.md`). Snapshots and MJPEG recording do not use it. `h264_encoder.py` runs one `ffmpeg.exe` child (LGPL build pinned in `build_scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.py`; beside `RobotControl.exe` when packaged, `build/vendor/ffmpeg` in a checkout) whose output every viewer shares: 640×480 raw BGR in on stdin, at most one frame waiting (a newer frame replaces it); libopenh264 Constrained Baseline, one-second GOP, no B-frames; FLV out on stdout, because FLV tags carry each packet's size and keyframe bit (a packet is passed on about 2 ms after its frame; raw H.264 would only reveal a frame's end when the next one starts). Packets are converted back to Annex-B, keyframes carrying SPS/PPS. The child is in a Job Object with kill-on-close, so it cannot outlive RobotControl even if ffmpeg hangs; `stop()` terminates it (closing a pipe a writer is blocked in can hang on Windows). `LiveStreamingService._update_encoder` is the only owner of start/stop: the encoder runs while at least one viewer is connected and visible, and stops when the last one stops, disconnects, hides its tab or the service shuts down. The distribution loop paces camera frames to the encoder's rate (all 15 per second at level 0; every second or third frame when degraded). A crash sends viewers "Live view stopped unexpectedly and is restarting. Recording is not affected." and restarts after 1, 2, 4 … 30 s; a missing `ffmpeg.exe` makes session creation return 503 with the reason.

Each frame is one binary WebSocket message: an 18-byte header (`FRAME_HEADER`, `<BIdHHB`: version 2, sequence, capture time, width, height, flags with bit 0 = keyframe) followed by one Annex-B access unit. Errors and status remain JSON text. The browser acknowledges each decoded frame (`{"type":"ack","parameters":{"sequence":n}}`, cumulative); frames in flight are sized to each viewer's link (`StreamingSessionHandler.window`): about one round trip of video, `ceil(fps × smallest round trip of the last 10 s × 1.5) + 1`, at least 2 (also before the first measurement), at most one second of frames and 256 kB. The smallest round trip reflects distance rather than the queueing our own frames cause on a thin link, so a far but fast link (the tunnel from another site) gets every frame while a slow one cannot build a queue. (Until October 2026 the window was a fixed 2 frames, which allowed only two frames per round trip: above about 130 ms the H.264 chain broke every second and the picture froze until the next keyframe.) Because a delta frame needs every frame since its keyframe, frames waiting for the window queue briefly (`pending`): a keyframe replaces the queue; a delta arriving while the oldest waiting frame is over 0.5 s old or 8 frames wait, or while the viewer joins, resumes or after a send failure, makes that viewer skip to the next keyframe (at most a second). A slow link therefore lowers that viewer's frame rate with bounded delay, without slowing anyone else; measured in `test_frame_delivery.py` and `performance_probe.py --rtt`. A viewer that acknowledges nothing for `ACK_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (15) is ended. `pause`/`resume` come from a hidden/visible browser tab; pausing clears the in-flight count and the waiting frame. The browser also sends `keepalive` every 30 s (paused or not); a connected session whose browser has sent nothing for `BROWSER_SILENCE_SECONDS` (75) is ended, so a half-open connection cannot hold a session slot, and Cloudflare (closes WebSockets idle for about 100 s) keeps a paused tab connected. The CPU guard (`_apply_resource_guard`) samples RobotControl's summed CPU **plus the ffmpeg child's** (ffmpeg converts and encodes in its own process; 75 % means three quarters of one core). At or above the soft limit for 2 samples it steps the shared encoder down one level per second through `encoder_levels` in `config.py` (15 fps/600 kbit/s, 7.5/300, 5/200: each rate divides the 15 fps capture so frames stay evenly spaced, and the GOP is `int(fps)` frames, at most a second; each change restarts the encoder at a keyframe); 10 consecutive samples below 50 % step it back up; at or above the hard limit for 3 samples it ends all sessions (and so the encoder). There is no per-viewer quality: the API's former high/medium/low/adaptive setting was removed. Do not normalise the CPU figure per core: that would loosen both protections fourfold on the N100.

## Selecting and connecting a device

DirectShow enumeration reads friendly names and device paths without opening cameras. Numeric indexes are temporary. The selection is saved in `data/config/camera_selection.json` (swapped in with `utils/filesystem.replace_file`, so a scanner reading it cannot fail a reselection), then resolved and checked around every camera open. Missing/ambiguous identities require explicit selection. Identical model names are distinguished in the UI by the current device number. Changing USB ports can change identity and require reselection.

On a new installation, automatic startup uses the configured primary index only when it can resolve a unique identity. A missing camera leaves automatic recording waiting. Refresh/select/connect after attaching it; there is no retry loop. First selection can retain waiting recording intent. Changing an existing selection requires stopping recording. Reconnect preserves recording intent; reconnect after an intentional stop supplies preview only.

## API and permissions

All paths below are beneath `/api/camera`:

- `GET /control-status`: authenticated, cached devices, health and operation state. No hardware probing.
- `POST /devices/refresh`: admin-only device enumeration.
- `PATCH /selection`: admin-only, JSON `{ "device_identity": "..." }`.
- `POST /connect`, `/reconnect`, `/recording/start`, `/recording/stop`: admin-only operations.

Operations return HTTP 202 with `data.operation.id`. Poll control-status for pending/succeeded/failed and errors. Overlapping operations return 409. The operation revision distinguishes a newer operation initiated by another administrator. Existing numeric recording APIs remain adapters and run blocking operations off the API event loop. The legacy public health endpoint retains limited fields; detailed identities require authentication.

Capture state, recording state, automation state and viewer connection are separate. A live process is not proof of recording. `CAMERA_CONFIG.no_frame_seconds` defaults to 10; `startup_seconds` defaults to 20. Both use monotonic time. Repeated images count as frames and are not analyzed for scene changes. No camera failure changes Hamilton execution status or triggers completion callbacks.

## Clips and storage

Keep the existing 640x480 capture request, 15 FPS camera request (`CAMERA_CONFIG.capture_fps`; 30 until October 2026, when live view and recording used at most 15 and 7.5 of the 30 decoded frames, and the dark enclosure needed the longer exposure a 15 fps camera may use; `docs/plans/live-view-low-light.md`), MJPEG AVI recording, measured rolling recording rate capped at 7.5 FPS, one-minute clips, 120-clip rolling limit and 15-minute archive window. Do not change quality as part of recovery work.

Finalized clips are then stored as H.264 MP4 (`clip_transcoder.py`; measurements and brief in `docs/plans/h264-rolling-clips.md`). The camera helper always writes MJPEG AVI, so recording never depends on ffmpeg. After each clip event, one ffmpeg child (bundled `ffmpeg.exe`, `libopenh264`, `CAMERA_CONFIG.clip_h264_kbps`, default 1000) at BelowNormal priority inside a kill-on-close Job Object converts clips newest first, one at a time, to `<stem>.mp4.tmp`. Decoding, filtering and the verifying decode run with one thread each (input-side `-threads 1`, `-filter_threads 1`; the encoder already had `-threads 1`). FFmpeg still runs its stages side by side, so a conversion peaks at about 1.5 cores for 1–3 s. On 8 real clips on the development PC (i5-12490F VM; not the N100) it cost 1.24 CPU-s per clip instead of 1.50 with default thread counts, about 2 % of one core averaged over the minute; the verifying decode no longer bursts over several cores, and it takes 0.3 s longer. Pinning ffmpeg to one core caps the peak but cost 50 % more CPU-seconds, so it is not used. The MP4 replaces the AVI only after it decodes to exactly the sidecar's frame count; the swap, the sidecar `path` update and the in-memory clip list change happen under `clips_lock`, which the archive holds while copying. The MP4 keeps the AVI's modification time. Clips without a sidecar, `.partial.avi` files and clips that failed in this run stay MJPEG; failures are retried after a restart. A clip briefly has both formats when the AVI was open (a download) or RobotControl stopped between the rename and the delete: an MP4 exists only after verification, so the next pass removes the AVI. `finalized_clips()` lists one file per clip (MP4 preferred) and `clip_files()` returns every format of a clip; listing, cleanup and storage statistics use them. Before an experiment archive copies its window, `wait_for_stored_clips` waits up to 60 s for those clips' conversion; on timeout it archives the MJPEG.

`control-status` reports `clip_storage`: `state` (`idle`, `transcoding`, `unavailable` when ffmpeg.exe is missing), `pending`, `transcoded`, `failed` and `last_error` (the latest per-clip failure; the missing-ffmpeg message clears once ffmpeg is found again). Each conversion logs `Rolling clip stored as H.264` (sizes, seconds); each failure logs `Rolling clip kept as MJPEG` with the reason. Measure size and CPU on a machine with `backend/scripts/clip_transcode_probe.ps1`.

Active or interrupted files end in `.partial.avi`. They are excluded from normal cleanup, archive selection and finalized downloads. They remain available for operator investigation; do not label them complete or delete them automatically. Completed new clips have JSON sidecars containing actual frame counts, elapsed duration, device identity and generation. Legacy clips without sidecars keep unknown counts rather than invented values. Cleanup removes every format and the sidecar of expired finalized clips.

Archival still follows Hamilton completion, including existing run association and paused-state rules. Registering the same completion callback repeatedly must remain idempotent. A camera reconnect must not register duplicate archival callbacks.

Recording downloads support GET/HEAD and byte ranges for resumable remote downloads. Preserve archive directory naming and existing API envelopes. Runtime configuration is separate from SQLite; this change requires no database migration.

## Troubleshooting and validation

Check control-status capture state, frame age, last-write age, heartbeat age, read-failure count, generation and operation error. Each connection logs `Camera capture | … | format=… | requested_fps=15 | reported_fps=…` (the format the driver negotiated, MJPG or YUY2) and, 10 s later, `Camera capture | … | delivered_fps=…` counted from frames that actually arrived; a driver may ignore the request or lower the rate in the dark. To return to the earlier request set `capture_fps` to 30 (the encoder levels divide both); the revert steps are in `docs/plans/live-view-low-light.md`. Recording measures its own rate at connection (capped at 7.5) and live view takes what arrives up to its level's rate. A fresh heartbeat with old frames can mean a blocked capture/writer call. A repeated still image alone is inconclusive. Opt-in resource diagnostics include these counters without recording image payloads.

For clip storage run `uv run --locked python backend/e2e/clip_transcode_check.py` (real ffmpeg, disposable clips; `--clips <folder>` adds real footage). Run `uv run --locked python -m pytest backend/tests/test_camera.py backend/tests/test_camera_worker.py backend/tests/test_camera_control_api.py backend/tests/test_automatic_recording.py backend/tests/test_camera_download_api.py`, and for live view `backend/tests/test_frame_delivery.py backend/tests/test_streaming_lifecycle.py backend/tests/test_h264_encoder.py` (the last needs `build_scripts/fetch_ffmpeg.py` once). Tests must mock device enumeration and use temporary storage. Never run hardware probes through a production CameraService instance.

Windows packaging must retain early `multiprocessing.freeze_support()` before application imports. Verify packaged recording and reconnect; source tests alone do not exercise frozen child startup. Preserve the previous package and runtime data. See `docs/camera-recovery-validation.md` for measured results and outstanding physical/endurance checks.
